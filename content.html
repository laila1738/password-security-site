Passwords and MFA

Start with the password, then add the second factor.

What makes a password strong

  • Long: a passphrase of several random words beats a short complex password.
  • Unique: a different password for every account.
  • Unpredictable: no names, birthdays, or keyboard patterns like qwerty.

Common attacks

AttackHow it worksBest defense
Brute forceSoftware tries many guesses until one works.Long, random passwords
Credential stuffingLeaked passwords are tried on other sites.Unique passwords and MFA
PhishingA fake page or message tricks you into typing your password.Check the link, use MFA, never share codes

Password managers

A password manager creates and stores a unique password for each account, so you only remember one strong master passphrase. It also helps you avoid typing passwords into fake sites.

How MFA works

MFA asks for something you know (password) plus something you have or are (phone, key, fingerprint).

Authenticator app

Generates short-lived codes on your phone. A good everyday choice.

SMS code

Easy to use, but weaker because phone numbers can be hijacked. Better than nothing.

Security key

A physical device you plug in or tap. Strong protection against phishing.

Never share a verification code with anyone, even someone claiming to be support.
